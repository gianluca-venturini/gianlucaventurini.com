Why the nozzle doesn't melt

The gas in the chamber is around 3,500 K. Copper alloys melt near 1,350 K. The wall survives because heat is pulled out of it faster than the flame can push it in.

Not to scale. The liner is only a few millimetres thick, which is the point: a thin, highly conductive wall can't build up much heat.

1

Regenerative cooling

Cryogenic methane runs through hundreds of small channels milled into the chamber and nozzle wall before it is burned. The fuel soaks up the heat, and that heat isn't wasted: it warms the methane on its way to the preburner.

2

Film cooling

The injector sprays extra fuel along the inside of the wall. It forms a cooler boundary layer so the hottest gas never touches the metal directly.

3

Copper that conducts fast

The liner is a copper alloy. Copper moves heat so quickly that the hot face stays close to the coolant temperature on the other side. A steel jacket outside carries the pressure loads.

The throat is the hardest spot: the gas is densest and fastest there, so heat flux peaks. Cooling channels are packed tightest around it.
